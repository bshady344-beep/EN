Definition

A paragraph is a group of related sentences that discuss one main idea.

How long?
1 sentence
10 sentences

The number of sentences is unimportant. The paragraph just has to be long enough to develop the idea.

On its own. For example, an answer in an exam: "Define management by objective, and give one example of it."
Part of a longer piece. Such as an essay or a book.

Part 1 of 3 · Top layer

The topic sentence

Definition

The topic sentence states the main idea of the paragraph. It names the topic and limits it to one specific area that can be discussed completely in a single paragraph.

Two parts, one sentence

The topic names the subject. The controlling idea limits it. Together they tell the writer what to include and the reader what to expect.

Topic+Controlling idea=Topic sentence

Gold, a precious metal, is prized for two important characteristics.

From the model paragraph "Gold". The reader now knows to look for two characteristics.

Three points to remember

Point 1

A complete sentence

It has at least one subject and one verb.

✗ Driving on freeways.
✓ Driving on freeways requires skill and alertness.

Point 2

Topic + controlling idea

It names the topic, then limits it to a specific area.

Online shopping saves busy people time and money.

Point 3

The most general statement

It gives only the main idea, with no specific details. Not too general, not too specific.

Test it in Paragraph 4.

Common mistakes

Check every topic sentence for a verb, a capital letter and a period.

No verb

Not thisSmall cafés in Alexandria very popular.

Write thisSmall cafés in Alexandria are very popular with students.

Only a topic

Not thisThe importance of English.

Write thisEnglish helps business students find good jobs.

An announcement

Not thisIn this paragraph, I will talk about my city.

Write thisMy city, Alexandria, is famous for its beaches.

Too many ideas

Not thisCairo is crowded, historic, and cheap.

Write thisCairo has many historic places to visit.

Where does it go?

At the beginning. The best and most common choice. Readers want to know what they will read about as soon as they begin.
At the end. Possible when the paragraph starts with examples or facts and then draws a conclusion from them.
Part 2 of 3 · The filling

The supporting sentences

Definition

Supporting sentences develop the topic sentence. They explain or prove it by giving more information: main points, examples and details.

How "Gold" supports its topic sentence

Two main points, and each one has its own example. The order matters, so read it from top to bottom.

  1. Main point 1 · beautyFirst of all, gold has a lustrous beauty that is resistant to corrosion.
  2. ExampleFor example, a Macedonian coin remains as untarnished today as the day it was made 25 centuries ago.
  3. Main point 2 · usefulnessAnother important characteristic of gold is its usefulness to industry and science.
  4. ExampleThe most recent use of gold is in astronauts' suits.

Transition words

They guide the reader from one supporting sentence to the next.

First of allFor exampleAnotherTherefore

What good support looks like

Tip 1

Back every point

Each main point gets specific examples: the Macedonian coin, the astronauts' suits.

Tip 2

Stay on one idea

Each controlling idea needs its own paragraph. One paragraph, one controlling idea.

Tip 3

Add information

Supporting sentences give more than the topic sentence did. They explain, they do not repeat.

Analyze the model

Tap a question to reveal the answer.

1 · What is the topic of the paragraph?
Gold.
2 · What two main points does the writer make about it?
Gold is beautiful (sentence 2) and gold is useful to industry and science (sentence 6).
3 · In which two sentences does the writer say there are two main points?
Sentence 1 ("two important characteristics") and sentence 10 ("not only for its beauty but also for its utility").
4 · What examples support each point?
Beauty: jewelry, coins and ornaments; a Macedonian coin still untarnished after 25 centuries.
Usefulness: photography and dentistry; gold-plated heat shields on astronauts' suits.
Part 3 of 3 · Bottom layer

The concluding sentence

Definition

The concluding sentence signals the end of the paragraph and leaves the reader with the important points to remember. Not every paragraph has one.

The last line of "Gold"

In conclusion, gold is treasured not only for its beauty but also for its utility.

It repeats the two points that sentence 1 announced: beauty and utility.

Useful ways to begin

In conclusion,In short,

Do I need one?

Stand-alone paragraph. A concluding sentence is customary, for example when you answer a test question.
Part of a longer piece. An essay or a book chapter usually does not need one in every paragraph.

The whole paragraph at a glance

Topic sentenceGold, a precious metal, is prized for two important characteristics.
Supporting sentencesBeauty (jewelry, coins) and usefulness (photography, dentistry, astronauts' suits)
Concluding sentenceIn conclusion, gold is treasured not only for its beauty but also for its utility.
